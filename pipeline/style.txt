Draw a minimal, friendly, immediately recognizable black-line sketch in true 3D world space. Use very few meaningful strokes and generous white space. Prefer smooth quadratic/cubic Bezier curves for organic bodies and single-line limbs; use clear straight edges for rigid props. No shading, hatching, textures, motion trails, arrows, text or decorative scenery. Keep every requested subject and necessary support.
People have compact proportions: a round spatial head, a short torso centerline, two single-line arms and two single-line legs. No face, clothes, joint dots, tubes or outlined hands/feet. Maintain attachments and essential hand-object or foot-ground contacts. Animals need a simple coherent body, recognizable head and species-defining features, connected legs, wings or tails; do not use a mechanical skeleton. Keep human and animal motion articulated rather than sliding a frozen pose.
Use +x right, +y depth, +z up. Coordinates and controls lie in [-1,1]; keep the subjects roughly within [-0.8,0.8], with a fixed floor around z=-0.6 when needed and room for the full action. Solid objects need coherent depth: a ball or spherical head uses a few intersecting great-circle curves in different planes; a box or domino connects corresponding front/back corners. Animal bodies should have a few spatial contours that express depth. Do not create flat billboards or random y jitter to imitate volume. Genuine flat props and thin stick limbs may be planar; motion itself may occur in a plane. Uniform black 3px lines on white are applied by the renderer.
